# 7 Warehouse Storage Tips Every Delhi Business Should Know

Warehouse inefficiency is one of the most expensive problems a business can have — and one of the least visible. Goods get damaged in storage, stock counts are wrong, and retrieval takes twice as long as it should. In Delhi's fast-moving commercial environment, these problems compound quickly.

Whether you run a retail distribution business, an e-commerce operation, or a manufacturing unit, these seven tips will help you get more out of your warehouse space.

## 1. Organise by Movement Frequency, Not by Product Category

Most businesses arrange their warehouse by product type — all electronics together, all textiles together. A better approach is to arrange by how often items move.

**Fast-moving goods** (daily or weekly dispatch) should be closest to the loading dock. **Slow-moving goods** (monthly or seasonal) can go to the back or upper shelves.

This single change can reduce picking time by 30–40% in a busy warehouse.

## 2. Use Vertical Space — Delhi Warehouses Are Expensive

Warehouse space in Delhi NCR — especially near Okhla, Noida, and Gurgaon — is not cheap. Most businesses use only 40–50% of their available cubic space because they stack goods only 4–5 feet high.

Invest in proper racking systems that go up to 10–12 feet. With the right forklift or reach truck, you can effectively double your storage capacity without moving to a larger facility.

## 3. Implement a Simple Barcode or QR System

You do not need expensive warehouse management software to track inventory accurately. A basic barcode or QR code system — even using free tools — can dramatically reduce stock discrepancy.

Each shelf location gets a code. Each product gets a code. Every inbound and outbound movement is scanned. This gives you a real-time count without manual stock-taking.

For small and medium businesses in Delhi, this is one of the highest-ROI investments you can make in your warehouse operations.

## 4. Establish a Clear Inbound and Outbound Process

One of the most common causes of warehouse chaos is goods arriving and leaving without a documented process. Drivers unload wherever there is space. Stock gets mixed with existing inventory before it is counted.

Set up a dedicated **inbound zone** where all incoming goods are received, counted, and verified before being put away. Set up a separate **outbound staging area** where orders are assembled before loading.

This two-zone approach prevents mix-ups and makes stock discrepancies easy to identify.

## 5. Schedule Regular Cycle Counts Instead of Annual Stock-Takes

Annual stock-takes are disruptive and inaccurate. By the time you count everything once a year, errors have compounded for months.

A better approach is **cycle counting** — counting a small portion of your inventory every week. Over the course of a quarter, you will have counted everything at least once, and you will catch discrepancies while they are still small and correctable.

## 6. Protect Against Delhi's Climate

Delhi's climate is harsh on stored goods. Summers bring extreme heat (45°C+), monsoon brings humidity, and winters bring dry cold. Each of these conditions can damage goods differently.

- **Electronics and pharmaceuticals** need climate-controlled storage
- **Textiles and paper goods** are vulnerable to monsoon humidity — use silica gel and ensure proper ventilation
- **Food products** need temperature monitoring year-round
- **Metal goods** can rust during monsoon — store on pallets, not directly on the floor
